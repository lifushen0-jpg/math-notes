## Problem 1. Why Is Normality Exactly the Condition Needed to Form a Quotient Group?

Let $H\le G$. Consider the set of left cosets $$G/H=\{gH:g\in G\}.$$ Attempt to define a multiplication on $G/H$ by $$(gH)(kH)=gkH.$$ Prove that the following three con ditions are equivalent:

1. $H\trianglelefteq G$.

2. The product $$(gH)(kH)=gkH$$ is independent of the choice of representatives $g$ and $k$.

3. The natural projection $$\pi:G\to G/H,\qquad g\mapsto gH$$ can be made into a group homomorphism.

Finally, explain why the meaning of normality should not be viewed merely as the identity $gH=Hg$, but rather as the condition that allows all elements of $H$ to be collapsed to the identity while preserving a well-defined group structure.

## Problem 2. What Does the First Isomorphism Theorem Really Say?

Let $$f:G\to H$$ be a group homomorphism, and let $$N=\ker f.$$ Without directly invoking the First Isomorphism Theorem, prove the following:

1. $$f(g_1)=f(g_2)\iff g_1N=g_2N.$$

2. There exists a unique homomorphism $$\overline f:G/N\to H$$ such that $$f=\overline f\circ\pi,$$ where $$\pi:G\to G/N$$ is the natural projection.

3. The induced map $$\overline f:G/N\to\operatorname{Im}f$$ is an isomorphism.

4. Explain why the First Isomorphism Theorem may be interpreted as saying that a homomorphism first forgets some information and then embeds the remaining information faithfully. Why is the forgotten information described exactly by $\ker f$?

## Problem 3. When Is a Group Really a Direct Product of Two Subgroups?

Let $$H,K\trianglelefteq G.$$ Assume $$HK=G,\qquad H\cap K=\{e\}.$$ Prove that $$G\cong H\times K$$ without directly invoking the internal direct product theorem. Proceed as follows:

1. Prove that every $g\in G$ can be written in the form $$g=hk,\qquad h\in H,\ k\in K.$$

2. Prove that this representation is unique.

3. Prove that $$hk=kh\qquad(\forall h\in H,\ \forall k\in K).$$ Hint: study the commutator $$[h,k]=hkh^{-1}k^{-1}.$$

4. Prove that $$\mu:H\times K\to G,\qquad(h,k)\mapsto hk$$ is an isomorphism.

5. Explain the role played by each of the three conditions $$HK=G,\qquad H\cap K=\{e\},\qquad H,K\trianglelefteq G.$$ In particular, if any one of these assumptions is removed, identify which step of the proof can fail.

## Problem 4. Chinese Remainder Theorem and Matrix Groups over $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$

Let $$m=ab,\qquad \gcd(a,b)=1.$$ The Chinese Remainder Theorem gives a ring isomorphism $$\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/b\mathbb Z.$$ Define $$\Phi:M_n(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\to M_n(\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z)\times M_n(\mathbb Z/b\mathbb Z)$$ by reducing each matrix entry modulo $a$ and modulo $b$.

1. Prove that $\Phi$ is a ring isomorphism.

2. Deduce that $$GL_n(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\cong GL_n(\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z)\times GL_n(\mathbb Z/b\mathbb Z).$$ Explain carefully why a matrix over $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$ is invertible if and only if both of its components modulo $a$ and modulo $b$ are invertible.

3. Prove that $$SL_n(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\cong SL_n(\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z)\times SL_n(\mathbb Z/b\mathbb Z).$$ You may use the determinant homomorphism $$\det:GL_n(R)\to R^\times$$ and the identity $$SL_n(R)=\ker(\det).$$

4. Define $$O_n(R)=\{A\in GL_n(R):A^TA=I\}.$$ Prove or disprove that $$O_n(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\cong O_n(\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z)\times O_n(\mathbb Z/b\mathbb Z).$$ Then determine whether the analogous statement holds for $$SO_n(R)=O_n(R)\cap SL_n(R).$$

5. Suppose $$m=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{\alpha_i}$$ is the prime-power factorization of $m$. Prove that $$GL_n(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\cong\prod_{i=1}^r GL_n(\mathbb Z/p_i^{\alpha_i}\mathbb Z).$$ Explain why this reduces the study of $GL_n(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)$ to the prime-power case.

## Problem 5. Why Do Quotients and Direct Products Have Opposite Universal Properties?

Let $$\pi:G\to G/N$$ be the natural projection for a normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$. Recall that if $$N\subseteq\ker f$$ for a homomorphism $$f:G\to H,$$ then there exists a unique homomorphism $$\overline f:G/N\to H$$ such that $$f=\overline f\circ\pi.$$ On the other hand, given homomorphisms $$f:K\to G,\qquad g:K\to H,$$ there exists a unique homomorphism $$\langle f,g\rangle:K\to G\times H$$ satisfying $$\pi_G\circ\langle f,g\rangle=f,\qquad\pi_H\circ\langle f,g\rangle=g.$$

1. Draw the corresponding commutative diagrams for the quotient group and the direct product.

2. Explain why the arrows in these two universal properties point in opposite directions.

3. Suppose another group $Q$ together with a homomorphism $$q:G\to Q$$ satisfies the same universal property as $G/N$. Prove that there exists a unique isomorphism $$G/N\cong Q$$ compatible with the natural projections.

4. Suppose another group $P$ together with homomorphisms $$p_G:P\to G,\qquad p_H:P\to H$$ satisfies the same universal property as $G\times H$. Prove that there exists a unique isomorphism $$P\cong G\times H$$ compatible with the projection maps.

5. Explain the meaning of the statement: “An algebraic object may be characterized not by what its elements are, but by how maps into or out of it behave.”
